Your Project Journey: Step-by-Step

It's a really exciting time getting ready to bring your new living space to life. However, we know it can also be a little daunting not knowing what's coming next and what you can expect to happen over the next few weeks. The breakdown below highlights the various stages involved in getting your project underway, so you're able to keep track of your progress throughout the process – and don't worry, we'll keep in touch to ensure you're fully up to speed with progress.

1. Sale Agreed
Contract signed and returned to us.

2. Survey Appointment
Our surveying team will contact you to make an appointment to take measurements, clarify details and ensure your design is suitable. You can discuss any potential changes at this stage. If your new living space requires planning permission, they'll also take the necessary measurements to produce the planning drawings.
Timescale: Approx. 2 weeks from point of sale.

3. Planning Permission
Your Surveyor will complete the planning application form (where applicable) and submit this with all relevant drawings to your local council for approval.
Timescale: This will vary according to your local authority, but please allow between 8-16 weeks from submission of application.

4. Survey Report
Working drawings and Planning Permission (where applicable) will then be sent to our pre-commencement team. Any necessary amendments to your sales contract will also be made at this stage by our Quantity Surveyor, by way of a contract variation.
Timescale: Approx. 1 week.

5. Test Dig Conducted
We conduct a 'test dig' at this stage of most installations. The purpose is to ascertain local ground conditions to ensure they are suitable for a traditional concrete base and foundation before any works commence.
Timescale: Approx. 1 week from submission of the survey report.

6. Building Regulations
Your Contracts Administrator will then submit your project (where applicable) for Building Regulations approval.
Timescale: Approx. 1 week.

Your Project Journey (Continued)

7. Customer Sign-Off
Our Contracts Administrator will contact you to sign off the final contract including any amends.

8. Procurement of Products and Sub-Contract Packages
At this stage, our procurement team will make the necessary arrangements to procure the relevant sub-contract packages for your build and order your products ready for building commencement.
Timescale: Approx. 2 weeks.

9. Commencement of Building Works
Our building team will make contact with you to arrange a building start date. They will discuss with you power and water requirements on site and will also discuss brick match with you before they start building your base.
Timescale: Approx. 1 - 2 weeks dependent on ground conditions.

10. Installation Commences
Once your building work is complete your frames and/or roof will be fitted. Your Project Manager will be your main point of contact from now until your installation is complete. Our team will liaise with you to agree suitable appointment dates for the various stages of work to be carried out.
Timescale: Approx. 1 week after the base completion.

11. Finishing Trades
Once your installation is complete, your Project Manager will contact you to arrange the relevant finishing trades including (where appropriate) plastering, plumbing, floor laying and electrics.
Timescale: Approx. 2 - 4 weeks.

12. Completion Appointment
One final inspection will be conducted to ensure that the installation aligns with both your expectations and our agreement. Upon completion, we will request that you sign our compliance declaration form. We will also carry out a complimentary builders clean on completion.

*Timescales provided are to act as a guide only and may differ depending on the complexity of the project.

Understanding Your Foundation: Piling

Every beautiful, long-lasting living space starts with what lies beneath it. Bringing your dream design to life is an exciting journey, and our first priority is ensuring your new extension or conservatory has a rock-solid, permanent foundation. Because every property is unique, we take a tailored approach to the groundworks beneath your home.

The Crucial "Test Dig"
At the beginning of every installation, we conduct a dedicated 'test dig'. The purpose of this is to ascertain your local ground conditions and ensure they are perfectly suitable for a traditional concrete base and foundation. We want to know exactly what we are building on before we lay a single brick.

[Insert Photo: Clean, prepared trench or structural piling]

What happens if the ground is unstable?
Sometimes, we discover that standard trench foundations are not suitable. Where the ground is found to be unsuitable, we will advise on a highly secure, engineered alternative, such as a specialist pile foundation. Ground can be unstable due to large nearby trees drawing moisture from clay-heavy soil, deep peat, loose sand, or previously backfilled areas.

What is Piling?
Instead of digging a wide, shallow trench, piling involves driving or drilling strong, narrow columns—made of reinforced steel and concrete—deep into the ground. These columns completely bypass the weak, unpredictable topsoil. They anchor directly into a much stronger, stable layer of subsoil or rock far beneath the surface.

Complete Peace of Mind
Piling provides the ultimate structural security for your property, ensuring your new space will not suffer from subsidence, movement, or cracking over time. Backed by complex structural calculations, it is the gold standard for protecting your investment.

Energy Efficiency: SAP Calculations

One of the most exciting aspects of adding a bespoke living space to your home is the flood of natural light it brings. However, when designing an orangery or a conservatory with expansive glass elements, it is vital to ensure the room remains comfortable all year round—warm in the winter and cool in the summer.

To guarantee this perfect internal climate, the government requires a specific thermal assessment known as a SAP (Standard Assessment Procedure) Calculation.

Why does my project need them?
UK Building Regulations state that if the amount of glass in your new extension exceeds 25% of its total floor area, you must legally prove that the new space will not cause excessive heat loss. Because your bespoke design features stunning, wide-spanning glazing to maximise your views, a SAP Calculation is required to satisfy your local Building Control department.

[Insert Photo: A bright Orangery interior bathed in sunlight]

How the technology works for you
A SAP calculation is a complex mathematical model of your home's energy efficiency. Our architectural team will calculate the thermal efficiency of your existing house and balance it against the high-tech materials we are installing, such as our multi-layered insulated roofing systems.

What do you need to do?
Absolutely nothing. Our in-house technical team and independent assessors manage the entire SAP Calculation process on your behalf. We seamlessly integrate these mathematical models into your Building Regulations application, ensuring your project moves forward smoothly without any stress or confusing paperwork on your end.

Your Project Journey: Full Planning Permission

Transforming your property is an exciting milestone, and we are here to guide you through every step. Because every house and every bespoke design is unique, some structures can be built under automatic rights, while others require formal Planning Permission from your local council. Based on our survey today, your beautiful new design will require a full planning application.

Why does my design require Planning Permission?
Local authorities have specific rules regarding how a property can be altered. Your project requires formal permission because it meets one or more of the following criteria:

  • Scale and Projection: The height, width, or outward projection of the design exceeds standard limits.
  • Property Location: Your home may be situated in a designated Conservation Area, a National Park, or it may be a Listed Building.
  • Boundary Proximity: The new structure may sit very close to a boundary line or front the main highway.

How we manage the process for you
Navigating local council bureaucracy can be daunting, so we handle it entirely for you. Our dedicated architectural team will draw up precise, scaled CAD plans of your existing house and the proposed new structure. We will then submit the full application to your local planning department and act as your official agent.

What are the timelines?
Timelines will vary according to your local authority, but please allow between 8 to 16 weeks from the submission of the application. While we cannot control the council's internal speed, we closely track the application every step of the way and will keep you fully updated.

Your Project Journey: Pre-Approved Planning

Transforming your property is a wonderful investment. Based on our meticulous survey today, there is great news: your bespoke design has been specifically tailored to fall within the government's "Permitted Development" rights. This means your project avoids the heavier complexities of a full planning application.

However, to guarantee total peace of mind and strict legal compliance, your project will still require a formal Pre-Approval document (officially known as a Lawful Development Certificate or Prior Approval).

Understanding the Pre-Approval Routes
While your design meets the strict criteria for automatic approval, local councils still require formal documentation to prove it.

  • The Lawful Development Certificate: Indisputable, official proof that your new living space is 100% lawful.
  • Prior Approval (Larger Home Extensions): A streamlined process allowing the council to formally verify dimensions and notify adjoining neighbours.

Why is this documentation essential?
If you ever decide to sell or remortgage your home in the future, buyers' solicitors will inevitably ask for legal proof that your extension was built compliantly. Having this certificate securely on file prevents costly delays and protects the total value of your home.

How we handle it

Protecting Your Home: What is a Cavity Tray?

When designing a new extension that joins seamlessly onto your existing house, ensuring the structure is completely watertight is our highest priority. The Great British weather can be unpredictable, and we want to ensure your beautiful new interior is permanently protected from the elements.

Before the building commences, one of our team will discuss with you the option of a cavity tray.

[Insert Photo: The 'Cavity Tray' cross-section graphic]

What is a Cavity Tray?
Most modern homes are built with two 'skins' of brickwork with a gap (a cavity) between them. When it rains heavily, water can soak into the outer brickwork and run down the inside of that cavity.

A cavity tray is a series of high level damp proof courses that bridge a wall cavity to direct moisture to the external face of a wall where the extension roof joins the house wall. Essentially, a cavity tray prevents moisture that penetrates the outer wall from appearing inside your new extension.

Why might my design need one?
In the event that your new living space is particularly exposed to prevailing winds and weather, we may recommend adding a cavity tray. This is highly relevant if your home is situated on the coast, in a rural location, or on top of a hill.

What if I choose not to install one?
In many instances, a cavity tray may not be required, but please speak to your building team for more information. However, please be aware that if you are advised that your property requires a cavity tray and you decide not to fit one, some water ingress may occur in severe weather conditions.
